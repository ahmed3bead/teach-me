# Retention and adaptation

Use this for multi-lesson journeys. Learning is not complete when content has been shown once.

## Retrieval and spacing

After meaningful learning, schedule the next check only when continuity and timing are available. Choose intervals from the learner's goal, difficulty, evidence, forgetting, and deadline; do not impose a universal fixed schedule.

Reviews should require retrieval before restudy. Mix:

- a brief recall or reconstruction;
- discrimination between similar concepts;
- application to a fresh but comparable case;
- cumulative questions that connect earlier objectives;
- delayed transfer for important capabilities.

Do not repeat the identical item as proof of retention. If retrieval fails, reduce the interval, repair the specific gap, and test again later. If it succeeds easily more than once, increase the interval and reduce support.

## Adaptive learner model

Store observations with context, evidence, confidence, and expiry rather than permanent learner labels. Useful observations include:

- prerequisite demonstrated or uncertain;
- terminology already understood;
- amount and type of help needed;
- representations that helped in this context;
- recurring misconception and whether it was repaired;
- pace, time, device, accessibility, and budget constraints;
- confidence-performance mismatch;
- review history and next evidence due.

An observation may guide the next lesson but must remain correctable. Recheck stale or contradictory observations.

## Adaptation policy

Change one important dimension at a time so its effect is observable: step size, representation, example context, language, amount of guidance, task authenticity, or retrieval interval. Preserve the objective unless evidence shows it is inappropriate or the learner changes the goal.

Explain consequential plan changes briefly. Never optimize for completion streaks, response length, or learner praise at the expense of learning.
