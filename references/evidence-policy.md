# Evidence policy

## When to research

Research claims that are recent, changing, specialized, disputed, consequential, or unfamiliar. Stable foundational teaching may use established knowledge, but cite core references when the learner asks or when verification improves trust.

## Source priority

1. Original research, standards, laws, datasets, and official documentation.
2. Government, academic, and recognized professional institutions.
3. Systematic reviews and maintained reference works.
4. Reputable expert secondary sources.
5. Community sources for lived experience or practical context, clearly labelled.

Popularity is not evidence quality. Prefer the source that directly supports the exact claim.

## Claim checks

Before teaching a consequential claim, verify:

- the source says what the lesson claims;
- scope, population, jurisdiction, version, and date match;
- omitted limitations would not change the meaning;
- inference is labelled as inference;
- meaningful disagreement is represented;
- links point to the supporting page, not a search result.

Use these confidence states: `verified`, `corroborated`, `inferred`, `disputed`, or `unverified`.

## Correction protocol

If an error is discovered: acknowledge it plainly, isolate the affected claim, provide the corrected claim and evidence, revisit dependent teaching, record a reproducible regression case, and avoid claiming the incident is resolved until checked.
