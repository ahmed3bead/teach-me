# Broad research sweep

Use this workflow before substantial teaching whenever the learner names a book, field, course, tool, framework, standard, or other defined subject.

## Purpose and boundary

Build a sufficiently broad, current, and traceable knowledge base for the learner's goal. “Broad” means representative coverage across relevant source types and viewpoints, not a claim that every source on the internet was found. Stop when important topics, disagreements, versions, and learner-relevant gaps are covered and further searching is unlikely to change the lesson materially.

Research may discover both paid and free sources. Never bypass authentication, paywalls, DRM, robots restrictions, platform terms, or access controls. For inaccessible paid material, record only lawful public metadata, previews, samples, syllabi, reviews, creator statements, and citations. Do not claim its closed content was inspected. Prefer an authorized learner copy when fidelity to that exact source matters; otherwise build a clearly independent synthesis from accessible authoritative sources.

If supplied public metadata and accessible authoritative results already establish a safe first objective, use them directly. Distinguish the inaccessible product from the independent synthesis, then deliver the compact path or first verified unit in the same response. Do not repeat the search, ask the learner to restate known context, or defer all useful teaching behind optional setup questions.

## Establish the research target

Resolve only choices that change the sweep:

- the exact subject, edition, version, jurisdiction, or date range;
- the learner's practical goal and current demonstrated ability;
- preferred teaching language and acceptable source languages;
- desired depth, time budget, and whether comparison is needed.

For an ambiguous title, disambiguate before researching. For a fast-changing subject, define a freshness cutoff.

## Search across source families

Seek a useful mix where relevant:

1. official documentation, publisher or author pages, standards, specifications, release notes, and original datasets;
2. books, papers, university courses, lecture notes, and research repositories;
3. complete courses, videos, playlists, talks, podcasts, transcripts, and demonstrations;
4. high-quality tutorials, technical articles, chapter notes, worked examples, and reference implementations;
5. exercises, labs, quizzes, flashcards, projects, and assessment material;
6. practitioner postmortems, issue trackers, engineering blogs, and community discussions for real-world failure modes;
7. paid offerings discovered through their lawful public descriptions, syllabi, samples, outcomes, and independent reviews.

Do not force irrelevant source families into every topic. Search in the learner's language and in other high-value source languages, then teach in the requested language.

## Inspect and score

For every candidate worth retaining, record:

- title, creator or institution, URL or location, publication or update date;
- source family, language, price/access state, edition or version;
- what was actually inspected and coverage depth;
- authority, directness, accuracy signals, recency, completeness, teaching quality, and practical value;
- conflicts of interest, missing prerequisites, known limitations, and best role in the learning path.

Do not use popularity or search rank as a proxy for accuracy. A polished summary may be useful for review but insufficient as the factual backbone.

## Synthesize the knowledge base

Create a topic map that separates:

- consensus foundations;
- version-specific or edition-specific changes;
- meaningful disputes and alternative models;
- prerequisites;
- practical patterns and failure modes;
- source gaps or inaccessible areas;
- recommended sources for explanation, verification, practice, and deeper study.

Map important claims to sources in the claim ledger. Map learning objectives to the topic map and practice material. Keep source-derived content, external verification, and teaching adaptation distinguishable.

## Teaching gate

Begin substantial teaching only when:

- the subject and relevant version are resolved;
- primary or authoritative anchors support the core;
- at least one independent source checks consequential claims;
- practical or assessment material exists for the current objective;
- important conflicts, gaps, and inaccessible coverage are disclosed;
- the source set is appropriate to the learner rather than merely large.

If the gate fails, continue research, narrow the objective, or explain the limitation. Research breadth must improve instruction, not delay a learner indefinitely; a small verified first unit may begin while later units remain under progressive research.

## Refresh

Store the sweep date and freshness needs. Re-check current documentation, standards, prices, laws, product behavior, and course availability when resuming after a meaningful interval. Do not repeatedly research stable foundations without a reason.
