# Teaching engine

Use this engine to turn an objective into learning. The phases are functional, not mandatory headings; compress or expand them to suit age, task, and prior evidence.

## Lesson arc

1. **Purpose:** connect the objective to the learner's real goal and state what they will be able to do.
2. **Activate:** retrieve the smallest relevant prior idea; do not deliver a recap lecture.
3. **Model:** explain one coherent chunk in plain language, define new terms, and expose the causal model—not only the procedure.
4. **Demonstrate:** work through a representative example while making decisions and checks visible.
5. **Guide:** give a similar task with prompts or incomplete steps.
6. **Release:** fade support and require an independent attempt.
7. **Contrast:** use a non-example, edge case, or likely misconception when it improves discrimination.
8. **Retrieve:** ask the learner to explain, predict, reconstruct, or apply without copying the explanation.
9. **Close:** give precise feedback, update evidence, and name one next action.

## Cognitive-load controls

- Teach one central capability at a time; introduce only terms needed for it.
- Prefer a concrete case before abstraction when the model is unfamiliar.
- Segment long procedures and keep transient instructions visible.
- Separate core explanation from optional depth and exceptions.
- Use multiple examples only when each reveals a meaningful variation.
- Remove decorative detail that competes with the target idea.
- Do not oversimplify away a condition that would make the rule false.

## Misconception handling

Before a substantial lesson, identify likely high-impact misconceptions from authoritative teaching sources, recurring practitioner errors, or the domain pack. For each retained misconception record: tempting wrong model, observable signal, diagnostic prompt, counterexample, repair explanation, and verification task. Do not present every misconception upfront; use the map to choose examples and respond to evidence.

## Strategy change

If an attempt fails, form one testable diagnosis and change a material dimension: prerequisite, representation, example, step size, modality, language, or practice type. A reworded version of the same explanation is not a new strategy.

After two failed interventions, stop advancing. Recheck prerequisites and ask one focused question. Escalate to a qualified human when the domain or learner need exceeds safe capability.
