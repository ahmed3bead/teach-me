# Pedagogy basis and limits

Teach Me is evidence-informed, not a claim that one teaching method works for every person or subject. The skill turns broad findings into conservative operating rules, then adapts from observable learner work.

| Design rule | Evidence anchor | Implementation in Teach Me | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Space important learning and retrieve it again | The US Institute of Education Sciences practice guide recommends spacing learning over time and using quizzing with active retrieval. | Retention requires fresh, unsupported retrieval at least 24 hours after earlier independent evidence. | The 24-hour floor prevents instant retention claims; it is not asserted to be the ideal interval for every objective. |
| Explain, model, then fade help | The same IES guide recommends combining graphics with verbal descriptions, connecting abstract and concrete representations, and alternating worked examples with problem solving. | Lessons use a causal explanation, worked example, scaffolded practice, then independent and transfer tasks. | Representation and fading still require domain judgement and learner feedback. |
| Use assessment to change instruction | Black and Wiliam's review reports evidence that strengthening formative assessment can improve learning. | Checks occur at meaningful boundaries, diagnose the error, and change the next explanation or task; a score alone is not the goal. | The review covers classroom research and does not validate every automated grader. |
| Offer multiple accessible ways to engage and respond | CAST's UDL Guidelines frame barriers as features of educational design and organize options around engagement, representation, and action/expression. | The learner may request another representation or decline routine checks; inaccessible formats require an equivalent route or an explicit hard block. | UDL is a design framework, not proof that arbitrary preference-matching improves learning. Teach Me does not infer fixed “learning styles.” |
| Treat transfer and delayed performance as stronger than recognition | The IES guide distinguishes remembering facts from using learning in new situations, while retrieval research shows delayed testing can support retention. | Progress states distinguish recognition, application, transfer, and delayed retrieval and record support level and task identity. | Real-world competence may need supervised performance, standardized measures, or a qualified assessor beyond this skill. |

## Primary and authoritative references

- Pashler, H. et al. (2007), [*Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning*](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/1), Institute of Education Sciences practice guide.
- Black, P. and Wiliam, D. (1998), [“Assessment and Classroom Learning”](https://doi.org/10.1080/0969595980050102), *Assessment in Education*.
- Roediger, H. L. and Karpicke, J. D. (2006), [“Test-Enhanced Learning”](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x), *Psychological Science*.
- CAST (2024), [UDL Guidelines 3.0](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/).

## What this evidence does not prove

- It does not prove that the generated explanation is factually correct; the source and claim ledgers address that separately.
- It does not prove that a model grader is reliable; model evaluations require named models, held-out cases, manual sampling, and recorded limitations.
- It does not justify diagnosing a learner, assigning a fixed style, or promising mastery.
- It does not replace a qualified educator or professional for high-stakes instruction.

Changes to the teaching contract should identify the rule affected, the supporting evidence or reproducible failure, the regression case added, and the rollback path.
